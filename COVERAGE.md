# Mathematical scope and coverage

This project formalizes **Replica symmetry breaking at arbitrary depth**, by **P. M. Aronow and Patrick Lopatto**, manuscript revision (16). It studies exact finite-step replica symmetry breaking in mixed even p-spin Ising models at positive temperature and zero external field.

The independent [Challenge](Challenge.lean) and proved [Solution](Solution.lean) select **Theorems 1.1 and 1.2**. Theorem 1.1 constructs a nonempty open coefficient phase at every finite depth, with a fixed increasing even exponent list beginning at four. The concrete interface gives exactly k+1 distinct support points, positive masses, and minimization against every finite Gaussian scheme of every competing depth. `ArbitraryRSB.full_measure_minimizer_iff_finite_schemes` connects this statement to the unique minimizer over **all probability measures on [0,1]**, using continuity and explicit finite grid approximations. The formal statement includes k=0, strengthening the paper’s k≥1 range.

Theorem 1.2 quantifies over every supplied admissible finite positive even polynomial covariance, including quadratic terms with ξ″(0)<1. From the paper’s nondegenerate atomic minimizer it proves positive physical entropy, one uniquely characterized critical coefficient in a closed transition window, its even-degree limit, and both exact depth equivalences. The seed hypothesis contains the actual minimizer property, the exact nonpositive first-variation contact set, and negative curvature at positive support atoms. It contains no atomic-Hessian assumption: the actual Hessian positivity is proved and then used internally.

Effective coefficients include inverse temperature squared and do not sum to one. The terminal function is log cosh; the functional includes a separate log 2; Gaussian variance increments are ξ′(q_next)−ξ′(q); and the Parisi correction has factor one half. Depth counts the actual support of a probability measure, rather than entries in a possibly redundant list.

## Representations, dependencies and proof routes

The original 415 `DepthPaper` modules retain covariances with degrees at least four and an internal nondegeneracy predicate that also contains a positive atomic Hessian. The active `GeneralDepthPaper` development permits degrees at least two, with subcriticality explicit. Revised bridges derive the internal Hessian invariant from the external paper predicate. The retained stronger hypotheses are not added to the revised seed-based results. The imported development used an earlier manuscript, SHA-256 `9d572803e6e3125dea11d8eb890d65d8eb5ce5eddd16e43430480b40dc5d1bba`; earlier comments and reports are not evidence for a differently numbered current claim.

Atomic coordinate arrays carry Lean’s product/sup norm. Closed coordinate balls and Hessian coercivity use that concrete norm. The manuscript does not specify its ball norm; an explicitly Euclidean restatement requires finite-dimensional norm-equivalence constants. The cutoff root is initially searched for in a larger interval, then proved to lie uniquely in the exact printed strip. The coupling window is the full printed symmetric window, and the high-overlap constant is literally 10/m0².

The potential uses the arbitrary-measure Parisi theory and a normalized variance clock. Exact CDF transport preserves atoms. Original-overlap-time PDE statements for arbitrary measures are weak/distributional, with absolutely continuous sections and almost-everywhere time derivatives; finite atomic endpoints use within-cell or one-sided time regularity. Every positive-order spatial derivative is jointly continuous and bounded on the full closed time strip. The potential itself grows like log cosh and is not globally bounded. The physical-time stochastic clause of Lemma 2.2 is proved for the paper's finite-support atomic domain. Its analytic curvature and martingale clauses have the stronger scope of arbitrary probability measures.

Theorems parameterized by `LimitingStandingData`, contact geometry, regularity or convergence data are conditional infrastructure. Definitions of these data are not existence proofs. The expanded weak-seed Section 4 and universal wrappers construct the required witnesses. Their actual scalar objective is P_(m²Ξ)(ν_m)/m+λm/2; the full mixed C³ conclusion follows from the envelope identity without assuming a C³ stationary branch. The all-finite-support temperature statements construct CDF partitions and do not assume an origin atom.

Several proof routes differ from the paper. The one-step endpoint law uses bounded backward Fourier tests, genuine Itô expectation and characteristic-function uniqueness instead of Girsanov. The closed-domain implicit-function theorem uses compact-fiber continuity, relative differentiation and smooth inversion rather than Whitney extension, and covers empty parameter domains and zero dimensions. The tanh comparison uses Gaussian reflection and bounded conditional-sign association. The entropy argument includes physical pressure, the Parisi formula, finite-volume Gibbs entropy bounds and convex derivative convergence. For Lemma 2.2, deterministic inverse-clock Wiener kernels construct a physical standard Brownian motion on the original sample space. Wiener transport identifies its square-root-volatility integral with the actual state driver. Inverse-clock partitions have vanishing physical mesh, and an L² comparison of the literal physical Hessian sums uses bounded Hessians and telescoping monotone volatility increments. Equality holds almost surely at each fixed time, and the gradient is proved to be a continuous modification of the resulting integral; no uncountable intersection of coordinatewise probability-one events is inferred. Brownian adaptation is proved modulo null sets in the original clock past, together with independent future increments; raw natural-filtration equality is not assumed. The four remarks are context. In particular, Remark A.8 has supporting all-positive mixed bounds, but the current Banach smoothness API is proved on mass (0,2); the entire all-positive remark is not separately certified as a theorem.
